Where it started, where it is

Igloo Hub began as an iPad estimator for the sales team. It is now a role-based platform covering the whole job from the lead landing in the office through pricing, the customer presentation, signature, production hand-off and commission. Seven people have their own login and see only the screens their job needs.

It runs offline, installs to the home screen like an app, and is deployed to Netlify. Everything below is working today unless the Open items section says otherwise.

  • PAR is a hard floor. The estimator will not price below it. There is no discount field and no approval queue — the floor is the control.
  • Complexity comes from facet count on shingles (2–4 Simple, 5–8 Moderate, 9–13 Complex, 14+ Very Complex) and from pitch on metal.
  • Squares tier changes at 24 squares. Under 24 prices higher per square.
  • Steep-pitch upcharge on metal runs from a 6/12 at $100 per square to a 24/12 at $240, scaled across the range.
  • Layer upcharge adds $65 per square at two layers and $115 at three, onto PAR on every package, so a tear-off cannot be sold at single-layer pricing.
  • Extras are added at cost. They raise the customer price but do not move PAR and are not commissionable.
  • One rate per package. The per-package upcharge set on 4 September is folded into the grid itself, so the figure an admin sees in Settings is the figure the estimate prices at — Sentinel Select $1,343 a square on a simple 8–23 square roof, Legacy $659, Foundation $445.

Commission

Everything above PAR is what the job earned. The rep's share of that rises with their signed volume for the calendar year, starting at 55/45 and reaching 90/10, resetting on 1 January.

Base pay is added on top and now varies by package, paid on each signed job and untouched by the tier ladder: Sentinel Select $300, Titan $250, Legacy $225, Fortress $200, Stronghold $175, Guardian $150, Foundation $100. It shows as a column of the pricing grid in Settings.

Reps do not see their tier percentage or their commission figure on the estimate. They see PAR, dollars above PAR, and the percentage over the floor. The full figures appear on their own commission worksheet and to management.

The estimator

  • Roofr report intake. A measurement PDF can be dropped in or a file chosen, and the squares, facets, eaves, rakes, valleys, hips and ridges pull through. Everything stays editable in the field.
  • Shingle accessories from the measurements. Starter bundles at 140 feet of eave and rake per bundle; hip and ridge at 30 feet per bundle, three bundles to the square.
  • Setup row — pitch from a 1/12 to 24/12 dropdown, existing layers, and a vaulted-ceiling flag, all carried onto the record. Two layers add $65 a square to PAR and three add $115, on every package.
  • Live sale price per square on every package, with an apply-to-all toggle that moves every shown package to the same percentage over its own PAR.
  • Packages sort dearest first, so metal sits above the shingle lines when added.
  • Extras — ice and water shield at $185 per roll covering two squares, high-temp at $235, OSB decking at $65 per sheet supplied and installed, plus siding trim. Collapsed until needed.
  • Roof length measurements moved to the foot of the sheet behind a toggle. Gauge and cover width appear only when metal is being quoted.

The customer presentation

A full-screen side-by-side comparison the rep hands to the homeowner. Five columns by default under the heading "Protect Your Biggest Asset", each with a product photo, the manufacturer logo, five selling points, the price and a Choose button. Selecting a package turns that whole column navy.

  • Colour selection per package from the real Southeast colour charts, with photographic swatches. Choosing a colour replaces that column's photo with the roof in that colour, and deselecting restores the default.
  • Spec sheet per package, opened from the column.
  • Financing screen the rep opens when the customer asks.
  • Signature capture, then a written confirmation with the CARES seal.
  • The layout auto-fits to the iPad it is running on, stepping photo, logo and bullet sizes down together so the Choose buttons are always reachable without scrolling.

Financing

All 21 Service Finance plans from the pricing matrix, with real plan numbers. Five lead plans show first — 2012, 4212, 4132, 4107 and 41232, which is marked as our best option — and the rest sit behind a show-all toggle grouped by family.

  • Payments calculate from the published payment factors. FEMA plans amortise after the promotional months. Same-as-cash plans show the balance due rather than a monthly figure.
  • The rep enters the amount to finance, defaulting to the full job price. Plans the amount does not qualify for grey out with the reason shown.
  • Every payment carries "Estimated payment, subject to credit approval."
  • Dealer fees are split 50/50. The rep's half comes off their above-PAR commission on the worksheet. The percentage is never shown to reps or customers — it appears only in admin Settings.

Residential inspection SOP

The full SOP as a working screen rather than a document: protocol, tools checklist, a mandatory photo counter, interior and exterior roof audits with Yes/No/N/A and remarks, an A–F grade sheet with a homeowner-facing view, and roof database code pickers. Audit answers suggest a grade the rep can override. Everything files against the appointment, so the grade travels with the record.

Leads

Cindy assigns leads into each rep's account. Reps see their own grouped as Past due, Today, This week, Later and No date set, with the appointment date on each card. From the lead a rep can call the customer, set the appointment, mark contacted, or start the inspection.

Records and the commission worksheet

A record is created when the rep presents and updated when the customer signs, numbered from CRC-1041. Reps see their own records with the company signed total above them. On a record they can reopen and re-price, duplicate for a second option, email the estimate to the customer, mark it lost with a reason, add a note, send it to Roofr, or delete it.

The worksheet replaces the paper form: weekly, ten jobs maximum. Signed jobs fill themselves in tagged from the record; anything not in the app is added by hand and tagged as such. Commission pays on sold squares at the contracted price. Every calculated figure can be typed over, and any hand-edited row names who changed it. Year to date, current tier and distance to the next tier sit across the top. The rep submits the week to Jennifer, who approves it for payroll or reopens it.

Management screens

  • Jobs board — six production stages, driven by signed records. Finance and production interlock on deposit approval.
  • Finance — deposits at 50%, commission payable, margin watch.
  • Office — leaderboard by above-PAR dollars, and production.
  • Performance — for David, Justin, Dawson and Jennifer. Three periods, sales figures, a per-salesperson breakdown, money, and the production pipeline by stage.
  • Settings — pricing grid editor, Service Finance plan table with dealer fees, team list, connections.

How it runs

  • Works offline. Records save to the device and survive a restart.
  • Installs to the iPad home screen and opens without browser chrome.
  • One layout for iPad side-by-side, desktop, and iPhone with stacked sections and bottom navigation.
  • Deployed to Netlify. Each build is versioned, so devices pick up updates on relaunch.
  • Tracking starts 1 September 2026. All demo data was cleared, and every screen computes from real records.
  • CSV export throughout, plus a weekly email hand-off to finance from both the Performance tab and the rep worksheet.

Open items

The one that matters most is the shared database.

  • No shared data between devices. Each iPad and computer holds its own records, so Jennifer only sees a worksheet submitted on her machine. The weekly email is the interim hand-off. Fixing this properly needs a backend and a developer.
  • Roofr is one-way. Signed jobs can be pushed to Roofr through a Zapier webhook. Leads cannot flow back into the app until there is a database for them to land in. Roofr has no published developer API — the one key it issues exists to connect it to Zapier.
  • CompanyCam photo pull is a placeholder pending OAuth.
  • Minimum-payment formula for Service Finance plans 1006, 1012 and 1018 is estimated at 1.75% of the balance. The matrix gives no factor.
  • Metal colour charts for SS26 and 29 Screw Down have not been supplied.
  • Guide content — objection handling, warranty details, the package talk track and who-to-call are stubbed, waiting on your words rather than invented copy.
  • Facet complexity bands await David's sign-off.
  • Vaulted ceilings are recorded but carry no upcharge. Say the figure and it prices like the layer upcharge does.
  • Guardian and Foundation carry no per-square upcharge, the ladder having ended at Stronghold.
