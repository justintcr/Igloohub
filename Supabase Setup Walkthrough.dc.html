Setting up the database

Fifteen minutes, all in a browser. No code to write. Your part is six steps; mine is everything after.
BEFORE YOU START
Do this on a computer, not the iPad. Have your work email to hand. Nothing here costs money — the free tier covers seven users comfortably.

Step 1 — Create the account

Go to supabase.com and click Start your project. Sign in with an email address or a GitHub account if you have one. Use a company email rather than a personal one — this account owns the company's data, and you want it to survive someone leaving.

Step 2 — Create the project

Click New project. Four things to fill in:

Nameigloo-hub
Database passwordClick Generate a password and save it in your password manager. You will almost never need it, but it cannot be recovered — only reset.
RegionEast US. Closest to North Carolina, and it keeps company data on US soil.
PlanFree. You can move up later without rebuilding anything.

Click Create new project and give it a minute or two to build.

Step 3 — Build the tables

In the left sidebar click SQL Editor, then New query. Paste in the script I will send you separately, and click Run. It creates six tables — records, leads, worksheets, worksheet rows, job status and team — and takes about a second.

You will see Success. No rows returned. That is what success looks like for this kind of script — it built things rather than looked something up.

Step 4 — Add the seven accounts

Click Authentication in the sidebar, then Add user → Create new user. Do this seven times, once per person, using their work email and a temporary password you give them.

Each person signs in with email and password once on their device. After that the four-digit PIN unlocks the app as it does now. This matters: it is what stops one rep reading another rep's records, and it is why the app can safely hold customer data on a server.

Step 5 — Copy the two keys

In the sidebar go to Project Settings → Data API. Two values to copy:

  • Project URL — looks like https://abcdefgh.supabase.co
  • anon public key — a long string starting eyJ...

Send me both. The anon key is designed to be public and is safe to put in the app — it is the accounts and the table rules that protect the data, not secrecy of that key.

DO NOT SEND
On the same page there is a service_role key. That one bypasses every security rule. Never put it in the app, never email it, never paste it into a chat.

Step 6 — Hand it back to me

I wire the app to your project, redeploy, and every screen starts reading and writing to the shared database instead of the device. Records, leads, worksheets and the jobs board all sync. The app keeps working with no signal — it writes locally first and pushes when the connection returns.

The honest risk

I cannot test against your live project from where I work — I can only verify the code is correct, not that your project is wired up right. So set aside an hour with me on Monday or Tuesday to shake out anything that misbehaves before Wednesday. Doing this with two days of slack is fine; doing it the night before would not be.

Worth knowing: once records live on a server, you are holding customer names, addresses and signed contracts off-device. Worth a word with whoever handles your insurance.
